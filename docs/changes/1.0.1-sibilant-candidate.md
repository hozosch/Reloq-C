# 1.0.1 Sibilant listening candidate

This candidate addresses reports of a grainy S above 11.025 kHz and of very
similar S timbres at 22.05, 32, 44.1 and 48 kHz. The public release remains 1.0
until the candidate has been evaluated by listeners. Audio listening packages
are supplied first; no new public add-on or release is published.

The existing noise-transfer target now broadens the dominant S resonance by
50% while retaining its centre. The existing power normalization retains the
integrated noise level. Variant C also anchors the lower noise flank to the classic 11.025-kHz
reference below 3.8 kHz, with a smooth blend to the wider target up to 5 kHz.
Symmetric broadening alone (variant B) increased lower noise energy, which
could worsen the newly clarified lisp-like impression. C reduces that lower
noise share relative to 1.0 at every extended clock.

This reduces concentrated spectral peaks and rapid
short-window energy variation. Those measurements are texture proxies, not
proof of an audible distortion or a perceptual quality score.

A smooth upper-band weighting increases with the output clock. Above 32 kHz,
the extra S resonance is fixed at 11 kHz with a 4.2-kHz width instead of moving
from 12 to 18 kHz with the clock. Its source band starts at 9 kHz. The aim is
a smooth, progressively wider S without increasing its main resonance peak.

The target fits into the existing causal noise filter. No extra per-sample
filter, audio buffer, resampler, synthesis cache or runtime dependency is
introduced. Numeric-frame durations, vowel synthesis and the 8/11.025-kHz
signal paths are preserved. SH and F controls retain their prior output.

The [numeric comparison](../validation/1.0.1-sibilant-texture.json) covers 84
steady-S configurations, with 24 exact low-rate S and 23 other exact control
comparisons. In the extended-rate cases the largest steady-S level change
is below 0.4 dB. Average one-ms envelope variation falls by about 7–22%.
The share above 5.5 kHz increases at each extended sample-rate step.

Listen to the same words and sentences at the same voice settings, first
against 1.0 and then across sample rates. Assess S smoothness, excessive
sharpness, S/Z balance and the release of TS. Numeric measurements cannot
guarantee an audible difference between every pair of adjacent sample rates
or establish which timbre listeners prefer.

`tools/build_sibilant_listening.py` creates lossless German and US-English
S/Z listening packages from the original and the two candidate libraries.
Individual WAVs retain their native rates. Guided comparisons use offline
48-kHz assembly, spoken labels and a common reference-derived gain for A/B/C
at each sentence/rate. Audio files are intentional listening deliverables;
the numeric tests still keep audio in RAM.
