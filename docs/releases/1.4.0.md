# ReLoq C 1.4.0

ReLoq C 1.4.0 improves optional word segmentation and pronunciation, extends the shared settings to all three platforms, and improves update detection. These changes are relative to 1.3.1.

## Word segmentation and pronunciation

- The optional unfamiliar-English-word and multilingual CamelCase options now use the common speech engine on NVDA, SAPI5 and Android. English compound handling supports both American and British English, and recognized compounds use their component pronunciations without an artificial word pause.
- The splitter protects established words such as “cupboard” and “breakfast” even when their spelling resembles a compound. Existing pronunciation dictionaries continue to take priority, and native spelling of capitalized words is preserved.
- English derivatives such as “switchable” and “searchable” retain their native pronunciation instead of being divided before the suffix.
- In NVDA, an object's name and role remain separate when segmentation is enabled, for example “YouTube window” and “YouTube document”.
- The built-in German dictionary pronounces “Cup” with a short a, including in compound words.
- Italian words with a final apostrophe, including “po'” and “po’”, no longer cause a crash. Their phonemes have been checked against the original Italian voice.

## Own dictionaries

Own Eloquence and NVDA speech dictionaries are managed in one list. The import dialog suggests the file format and keeps NVDA replacement text, regular expressions and entry options separate. Previously selected NVDA-format files are recognized on reload, so their numeric options are no longer spoken as part of the replacement.

SAPI5 and Android can also use NVDA speech dictionaries alongside Eloquence files, with language and voice selection and independently of the community dictionary switch. In NVDA, imported NVDA-format dictionaries are copied and can be edited in the add-on. SAPI5 and Android keep the selected source files linked; originals are not changed. Their own regular-expression engines may interpret some Python-specific patterns differently from NVDA.

## Android x86-64

Android now has a separate x86-64 package as well as the ARM64 package. A correction to the shared rule engine fixes missing speech on x86-64 Android. The update check selects the package for the installed architecture. Thank you to [Aaron Hewitt](https://github.com/aaron-gh) for the contribution in [pull request #35](https://github.com/hozosch/Reloq-C/pull/35).

## Updates

The update check compares the version and the build time. Once this version is installed, a newer build published under the same version number can be offered as an update without repeatedly offering the installed build. Updating from 1.3.1 uses the ordinary version comparison.
