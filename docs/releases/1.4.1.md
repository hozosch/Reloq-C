# ReLoq C 1.4.1

ReLoq C 1.4.1 improves word segmentation and dictionary-update feedback across NVDA, SAPI5 and Android. The changes below are relative to 1.4.0.

## Word segmentation and acronyms

- Words with internal capitals such as “BlindRadio” retain their CamelCase boundary when unfamiliar English word segmentation is enabled. The separate CamelCase option continues to work across languages.
- Plural abbreviations and acronyms such as “CDs”, “PDFs”, “URLs”, “PACs” and “SMSs” are handled more naturally. English-specific suffix rules do not add an English plural syllable to German “SMSs”. Both US and British English are covered.
- In NVDA, stock built-in dictionary rules no longer unnecessarily split protected acronyms or pronunciation dictionary entries. User-customized and add-on speech dictionaries retain priority, and punctuation and source positions are preserved. Other synthesizers keep their own dictionary processing.

## Dictionary updates

- **NVDA:** automatic community-dictionary updates announce the result without opening a modal dialog. A manually requested update still shows the entry and file counts in a dialog.
- **SAPI5:** the initial automatic download for the alternative dictionary source uses a temporary Windows notification instead of a modal dialog. A manually requested update still reports its counts in a dialog. Windows 10 may retain the notification in its notification center.
- **Android:** automatic source downloads complete without a success notification. Manual dictionary update and reload actions continue to show their results; failures remain accessible in the status field.

## Startup

NVDA can now restore a saved acoustic-timing setting before its graphical application is initialized. This fixes a specific first-start failure. Separate reports of fallback to OneCore and a crash while scrolling the Add-on Store could not be reproduced reliably and remain under investigation.

The NVDA add-on, SAPI installer and both Android packages update existing 1.4.0 installations in place and retain their settings and dictionaries.
